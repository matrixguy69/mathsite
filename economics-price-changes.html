Chapter 04 · Economics

Shifts, surpluses and shortages: why market prices change

The full chain-of-analysis toolkit: conditions that shift demand and supply, how markets return to equilibrium, and what governments do with maximum and minimum prices. Built around real past-paper questions from 2281.

CAIE 2281 IGCSE 0455

1. The big picture: shifts versus movements

Every "what happened to the market" question is really one question: did the curves move, or did buyers and sellers only move along the curves they already had? Get this distinction right and half the marks are already yours.

What changedOn the diagramCaused by
Movement along DQuantity demandedSlide up or down the demand curveOnly a change in the price of the good itself
Shift of DDemand itselfNew curve, left or rightIncome, population, tastes, related prices, expectations
Movement along SQuantity suppliedSlide up or down the supply curveOnly a change in the price of the good itself
Shift of SSupply itselfNew curve, left or rightCosts, technology, taxes, weather, number of firms
Exam trap"The price of coffee rose, so demand for coffee fell." Wrong. A price rise of the good itself causes a movement along the demand curve (a fall in quantity demanded), not a shift. Only a named condition from the table above shifts a curve.

2. The conditions that shift demand

Demand shifts when anything other than the good's own price changes. Learn the five conditions, because every demand-side exam question is testing whether you can pick the right one and name its direction.

ConditionChangeEffect on demandTypical exam example
IncomeIncome risesRight for normal goods, left for inferior goodsRising income, gold or luxury perfume: demand shifts right
PopulationPopulation risesRight (more buyers)Toothpaste, clothing, food: demand shifts right
Tastes and informationProduct becomes more popularRightA health report on tomatoes or bananas: demand shifts right
Price of related goodsSubstitute becomes dearerRightAir transport dearer: demand for sea transport shifts right
Complement becomes dearerLeftCar parking dearer: demand for cars shifts left
ExpectationsExpect future price risesRight (buy now)Buyers rush in before an expected rise
Exam tipWhen the question names an event ("a report says X is healthy"), translate it before you draw: which condition is this (tastes? income? related price?), and does it hit demand or supply? Write that translation down, it is usually worth a mark on its own.

3. The conditions that shift supply

Supply shifts when anything other than the good's own price changes. In practice, almost every supply question is about costs of production, because cost is what sits between any event and the firm's willingness to sell.

ConditionChangeEffect on supplyTypical exam example
Costs of productionCosts riseLeftWood dearer: supply of furniture shifts left; wage costs up: supply of shoes shifts left
Costs fallRightCheaper inputs: supply shifts right
TechnologyNew technique adoptedRight (cheaper, faster)Advances in technology raise supply
Taxes and subsidiesIndirect tax risesLeftTax adds to costs
Subsidy risesRightSubsidy cuts costs
Number of firmsMore firms enterRight (market supply rises)New firms open in the industry
Weather / natural eventsBad weatherLeft (agriculture)Prolonged bad weather cuts food supply
ExpectationsExpect future price risesLeft (hold back stock now)Producers wait for a better price
The one-step translationCosts rise → each unit is less profitable at every price → firms cut quantity supplied at every price → the whole supply curve shifts left. If your event can be squeezed into "costs rose" or "costs fell", you have your shift.

4. Chain of analysis: writing the diagram answer

Diagram questions ("analyse, using a demand and supply diagram...") are marked in a fixed order. Miss a link and the chain breaks. Every full answer runs through these steps:

  1. Translate the event into a named condition and say whether it hits demand, supply, or both.
  2. State the shift: demand (or supply) shifts right/left because [condition].
  3. Name the new equilibrium: the intersection of the new curve with the unchanged curve moves.
  4. State both price and quantity: equilibrium price rises/falls; equilibrium quantity rises/falls.
  5. Context sentence: finish in the language of the market ("so more steel is bought and sold at a higher price").
Worked example 1

Analyse, using a demand and supply diagram, the effect of an increase in income on the market for gold. (adapted from Q3(c)/21/M/J/20)

  1. Gold is a normal good, so higher income is a demand-side condition.
  2. The demand for gold shifts right: at every price, buyers now want more gold.
  3. Supply is unchanged, so the new equilibrium sits up along the original supply curve.
  4. Equilibrium price rises and equilibrium quantity rises.
  5. So the market for gold clears at a higher price with more gold bought and sold.

Demand shifts right; equilibrium price and quantity both rise.

Worked example 2

Analyse how advances in technology can affect demand and supply. (Q6(c)/22/M/J/17)

  1. Technology is a supply-side condition: new techniques usually cut costs of production.
  2. Supply shifts right, so at the original price firms are willing to sell more.
  3. The equilibrium price falls and equilibrium quantity rises.
  4. Demand itself does not shift, but quantity demanded rises in response to the lower price (a movement along D).
  5. If the technology also makes the product more attractive (new features, apps, functions), demand can shift right as well, which would push price back up and raise quantity further.

Supply shifts right (price down, quantity up); demand may also shift right if the technology makes the product more popular.

Worked example 3

Analyse the effect on the market for food of an increase in population combined with a prolonged period of bad weather. (Q4(c)/23/O/N/17)

  1. Two conditions at once: population is a demand condition, weather is a supply condition.
  2. More people means more buyers: demand for food shifts right.
  3. Bad weather damages crops: supply of food shifts left.
  4. Both shifts push the price up: demand up raises price, supply down raises price. Equilibrium price rises for certain.
  5. Quantity is ambiguous from the shifts alone: the demand shift raises quantity, the supply shift lowers it. In practice bad weather usually dominates, so quantity falls.

Equilibrium price rises definitely; equilibrium quantity depends on the relative size of the two shifts.

Exam tipWhen two shifts happen together, work out the effect on price and quantity one shift at a time, then combine. If the two effects on quantity push in opposite directions, say it is "uncertain" or "depends on which shift is larger". Examiners reward that honesty with full marks.

5. Surplus, shortage and market adjustment

Equilibrium is the price where quantity demanded equals quantity supplied. Push the price away from it and market forces (the "invisible hand") push it back.

Market stateHappens when price is...QD vs QSMarket responsePrice moves
Surplus (excess supply)above equilibriumQS > QD, unsold stock piles upFirms cut price to clear stockFalls back to equilibrium
Shortage (excess demand)below equilibriumQD > QS, buyers compete for goodsSellers raise price; some buyers drop outRises back to equilibrium
Worked example 4

Explain how market forces would respond to a shortage of drinking water. (Q6(b)/21/M/J/18)

  1. A shortage means quantity demanded exceeds quantity supplied at the current price.
  2. Buyers compete for the limited supply, so sellers can raise the price.
  3. Higher price has two effects: a movement along the supply curve (firms deliver more, including costly emergency sources) and a movement along the demand curve (some users cut back or find alternatives).
  4. Quantity demanded falls and quantity supplied rises until they are equal: the shortage is eliminated at a higher equilibrium price.

Price is bid up until quantity demanded equals quantity supplied; the shortage disappears at a higher equilibrium price.

The wording that scores"Market forces" or "the price mechanism" is not vague magic. Always name the two concrete movements: quantity demanded falls and quantity supplied rises as price adjusts. Those two clauses are the marks.

6. Linked markets: substitutes and complements

Some questions ask what an event in market A does to market B. The bridge is always the cross-price relationship.

Goods areEvent in market AEffect on market BExam examples
Substitutes (in consumption)Price of A fallsDemand for B shifts leftCheaper rail transport: demand for car transport falls
Price of A risesDemand for B shifts rightDearer air transport: demand for sea transport rises
Complements (used together)Price of A risesDemand for B shifts leftDearer car parking: demand for cars falls
Price of A fallsDemand for B shifts rightCheaper petrol: demand for cars rises
Substitutes in productionDemand for A rises (price of A rises)Supply of B shifts leftFarmers switch land from basketball shirts' cotton to football shirts
Worked example 5

Explain, using information from the extract and Figure 1, what happened to the market for sea transport as a result of the fall in the price of air transport. (Q1(f)/21/M/J/17)

  1. Air transport and sea transport are substitutes in consumption.
  2. The fall in the price of air transport makes air travel relatively cheaper, so some travellers switch away from sea transport.
  3. This shifts the demand for sea transport to the left.
  4. With supply unchanged, the equilibrium price of sea transport falls and the equilibrium quantity falls.

Demand for sea transport shifts left; both its equilibrium price and quantity fall.

Worked example 6

Analyse the effect of an increase in demand for football shirts on demand for basketball shirts. (Q5(c)/23/M/J/24)

  1. Football shirts and basketball shirts are substitutes in consumption for fans of both sports.
  2. Higher demand for football shirts (perhaps after a successful tournament) raises their price.
  3. Consumers see football shirts as relatively more expensive, so some switch to basketball shirts.
  4. The demand for basketball shirts shifts right, raising their equilibrium price and quantity.

Demand for basketball shirts shifts right, so their equilibrium price and quantity rise.

Exam trapWatch the direction the question gives you. If it says demand for A rises, consumers pay a higher price for A and switch to the substitute B. If it says the price of A falls, they switch away from B. Read which variable moved before you draw the arrow.

7. Introduction to maximum and minimum prices

Free markets do not always settle where governments want. Two interventions come up again and again.

Maximum priceMinimum price
Setbelow the equilibrium priceabove the equilibrium price
PurposeKeep essentials affordable (rent, food, water)Protect producers' incomes (farm wages, minimum wage)
Immediate effectQD > QS: a shortage (excess demand)QS > QD: a surplus (excess supply)
Side effectsQueues, rationing, black markets, lower quality, less incentive to supplyUnemployment (surplus of labour), unsold stock, wasted resources
EnforcementMust be policed: sellers want the higher market priceMust be policed: firms want to pay less / workers want jobs at lower pay
Worked example 7

Draw a demand and supply diagram to show how setting a maximum price below the equilibrium price will affect a market. (Q1(e)/21/M/J/23)

  1. Draw normal downward-sloping D and upward-sloping S, mark the equilibrium.
  2. Draw a horizontal line below the equilibrium, labelled Pmax, made compulsory with a dashed or highlighted line.
  3. At Pmax read off quantity supplied (on S) and quantity demanded (on D).
  4. QD exceeds QS, so mark the gap as a shortage (excess demand).
  5. Note the consequences: not everyone who wants the good can buy it at the legal price.

Pmax below equilibrium creates a shortage; the price cannot rise to clear the market.

Exam tipAlways label the intervention line (Pmax or Pmin) and the gap (shortage or surplus). An unlabelled dashed line earns nothing. If asked to "analyse", add one consequence: a maximum price can lead to black markets, a minimum price to unemployment or surplus production.

8. Practice set and answers

Do every question on paper first. Draw the diagram where asked. Open an answer only after you have committed to one.

Set A · Past-paper questions: shifts in demand

Analyse how a global recession could affect demand for sea transport. Q1(d)/21/M/J/17
A recession lowers incomes. Sea transport (especially for holidays and luxury cruises) is income-elastic: demand shifts left. For freight it is less clear, since firms may switch from dearer air to cheaper sea transport, shifting that demand right. Mention both sides for full marks; lead with the most likely effect.
Explain, using information from the extract and Figure 1, what happened to the market for diamond rings in 2015. Q1(f)/22/M/J/17
Diamond rings are a luxury, so the recession cuts demand (leftward shift). Demand shifting left with supply unchanged lowers both the equilibrium price and quantity of diamond rings traded.
Explain, using information from the extract and Figure 1, what happened to the market for food in 2015. Q1(f)/22/O/N/17
Food is a necessity: demand is income-inelastic, so a recession barely shifts demand left (unlike luxuries). Prices of basic food therefore stay relatively firm compared with luxury goods; any fall in demand is small.
Analyse, using a demand and supply diagram, the effect of an increase in income on the market for ice cream. Q1(c)/22/M/J/18
Ice cream is a normal good: higher income shifts demand right. New equilibrium: price rises, quantity rises. If ice cream were treated as inferior, demand would shift left instead; state your assumption.
Explain why the price of housing may increase. Q5(b)/22/M/J/18
Demand-side: rising population and incomes shift demand right. Supply-side: land is scarce and building takes time, so supply is inelastic and shifts little or left (rising construction costs). Rightward demand against fixed supply raises the equilibrium price of housing.
Analyse, using a demand and supply diagram, the effect a report stating that eating tomatoes is good for health will have on the market for tomatoes. Q3(c)/23/O/N/19
The report changes tastes in favour of tomatoes: demand shifts right. Supply unchanged in the short run. Equilibrium price and quantity of tomatoes both rise.
Analyse, using a demand and supply diagram, how a rise in income may affect the market for gold. Q3(c)/21/M/J/20
Gold is a normal luxury: demand shifts right. Equilibrium price of gold rises and quantity bought and sold rises.
Analyse, using a demand and supply diagram, how a greater awareness of the health benefits of eating fruit will affect the market for fruit. Q5(c)/21/M/J/22
Awareness changes tastes: demand for fruit shifts right. Price and quantity both rise in the new equilibrium. Supply can only respond over time (trees take years to mature), so the price effect is strong in the short run.
Analyse, using a demand and supply diagram, how an increase in income will affect the market for a luxury brand of perfume. Q3(c)/22/M/J/22
Luxury perfume is a normal good with high income elasticity: demand shifts right strongly. Equilibrium price and quantity both rise.
Analyse, using a demand and supply diagram, how the market for toothpaste would be affected by an increase in population. Q4(c)/23/O/N/22
More people means more buyers at every price: demand shifts right. Toothpaste supply adjusts slowly, so price rises a little and quantity rises.
Analyse, using a demand and supply diagram, how an increase in population size will affect the market for clothing. Q1(f)/22/O/N/24
Population is a demand condition: demand for clothing shifts right. New equilibrium: higher price, higher quantity of clothing traded.
Explain how improved education may affect the demand for cigarettes and for fresh fruit. Q2(b)/21/M/J/23
Education raises awareness of health risks: demand for cigarettes shifts left. The same awareness raises demand for fresh fruit, shifting it right. Two opposite shifts from one condition, judged by taste change.

Set B · Past-paper questions: shifts in supply and both curves

Explain, using information from the extract and Fig. 1, what might have happened to the market for steel in China in 2015. Q1(f)/23/O/N/17
China's construction slowdown cuts demand for steel: demand shifts left. At the same time, high-cost producers may cut output. A leftward demand shift with unchanged supply lowers price and quantity; if the question hints at rising production costs too, mention both curves and the price ambiguity.
Explain, using information from the extract and Fig. 1, what happened to the market for food in 2015. Q1(f)/22/O/N/17
See Set A. Key points: food demand is income-inelastic, so the recession barely shifts it; any supply-side weather effects would push prices the other way. Compare with luxury goods for full marks.
Analyse how advances in technology can affect demand and supply. Q6(c)/22/M/J/17
Technology cuts production costs, shifting supply right (price down, quantity up). It can also raise demand if the product becomes more useful or desirable (price up, quantity up). Always analyse both curves when the question says "demand and supply".
Analyse the effect on the market for furniture of an increase in the price of wood. Q4(c)/22/O/N/22
Wood is an input for furniture, so production costs rise: supply shifts left. Equilibrium price of furniture rises and equilibrium quantity falls.
Analyse, using a demand and supply diagram, how an increase in wage costs would affect the market for shoes. Q1(f)/22/M/J/24
Wages are a major cost in shoe production: costs rise, supply shifts left. Higher equilibrium price, lower equilibrium quantity of shoes.
Draw a demand and supply diagram to show the effect of a ban on burning stubble on the market for rice. Q1(e)/22/O/N/20
Burning stubble is a cheap way to clear fields; a ban raises farmers' costs, so the supply of rice shifts left. Mark a higher equilibrium price and lower quantity. Draw D downward, S upward, S2 left of S1, both equilibria marked.
Draw a demand and supply diagram to show how a report stating that bananas are good for health would affect the market for bananas. Q1(e)/22/O/N/23
The report changes tastes: demand shifts right (D2 to the right of D1). New equilibrium: higher price, higher quantity.
Analyse, using a demand and supply diagram, the effect on the market for food of an increase in population combined with a prolonged period of bad weather. Q4(c)/23/O/N/17
Demand shifts right (more people), supply shifts left (bad harvests). Both push price up. Quantity: demand shift raises it, supply shift lowers it, so state it is uncertain without magnitudes.

Set C · Past-paper questions: linked markets, adjustments, price controls

Explain how market forces would respond to a shortage of drinking water. Q6(b)/21/M/J/18
A shortage (QD > QS) bids the price up. As price rises, quantity demanded falls (conservation, alternatives) and quantity supplied rises (firms deliver more) until QD = QS at a higher equilibrium price.
Explain, using information from the extract, two reasons why a rise in the price of vanilla might not cause an increase in the price of ice cream. Q1(b)/22/M/J/18
(1) Vanilla may be a small share of total costs, so the cost increase barely shifts ice cream supply left. (2) Demand may be elastic or falling (substitutes, health concerns), so any supply shift cuts quantity more than price. Producers may also absorb the cost rather than pass it on.
Draw a demand and supply diagram to show how a rise in the price of car parking could affect the market for cars in the Philippines. Q1(e)/23/O/N/20
Parking and cars are complements. Dearer parking raises the total cost of owning a car: demand for cars shifts left. Equilibrium price and quantity of cars fall.
Draw a demand and supply diagram to show the effect of a decrease in the price of rail transport on the market for car transport. Q1(e)/22/M/J/23
Rail and car transport are substitutes. Cheaper rail makes driving relatively dearer: demand for car transport shifts left. Price and quantity of car transport both fall.
Analyse the effect of an increase in demand for football shirts on demand for basketball shirts. Q5(c)/23/M/J/24
Football shirts are substitutes for basketball shirts. Higher demand for football shirts raises their price; consumers switch to basketball shirts, so demand for basketball shirts shifts right. Price and quantity of basketball shirts rise.
Draw a demand and supply diagram to show how setting a maximum price below the equilibrium price will affect a market. Q1(e)/21/M/J/23
D downward, S upward, Pmax drawn below equilibrium. At Pmax, QD > QS: mark the gap and label it a shortage. The price is stuck below equilibrium and cannot clear the market.
Identify two reasons why someone may choose to train to become a dentist. Q3(a)/23/O/N/18
Not a diagram question, but it appears on this topic sheet: expected high income, job security, helping people, social status, or a taste for science. Any two clear reasons with a short explanation.
A government sets a minimum price for wheat above the free-market price. Explain one likely consequence. (own question)
At Pmin, QS > QD, so a surplus of wheat appears: unsold stock, government buying or storage costs, and resources wasted on wheat that nobody buys at the legal price.

9. Quick quiz

Click an option to check your answer.

MCQ 1 A rise in the price of coffee, other things equal, causes:

Answer: C. A change in the good's own price moves quantity demanded along the curve; it never shifts it.

MCQ 2 A bad harvest shifts the supply of wheat to the left. What happens to the equilibrium price and quantity?

Answer: B. A leftward supply shift against an unchanged demand curve raises price and cuts quantity traded.

MCQ 3 A maximum price is set below equilibrium. The market will show:

Answer: A. Below equilibrium, quantity demanded is larger than quantity supplied: excess demand, or a shortage.

MCQ 4 The price of cinema tickets falls. What happens in the market for streaming subscriptions (a substitute)?

Answer: D. Cheaper cinema tickets make streaming relatively less attractive, shifting demand for the substitute to the left.

MCQ 5 Population rises and a drought hits at the same time in a food market. The most certain prediction is that:

Answer: C. The demand shift and the supply shift both push price up. Quantity depends on which shift is bigger.

10. Exam checklist

  • Translated the event into a named condition (income, population, tastes, related price, costs, technology, weather) before drawing anything.
  • Said whether the event hits demand, supply, or both, and the direction of the shift.
  • Never shifted a curve for a change in the good's own price (that is a movement along).
  • Stated both the new equilibrium price and quantity in every diagram answer.
  • Finished with a context sentence in the language of the market.
  • For two simultaneous shifts, worked price and quantity one shift at a time, and said if quantity is uncertain.
  • Named the goods as substitutes or complements before linking two markets.
  • On max/min price diagrams: drew the line on the correct side of equilibrium and labelled the resulting shortage or surplus.
  • Mentioned a consequence of price controls (black market, queues, unemployment, surplus stock) when asked to analyse.
