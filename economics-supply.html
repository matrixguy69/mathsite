Chapter 02 · Economics

Supply: quantity supplied, movements and shifts

The supply chapter for CAIE 2281 and IGCSE 0455: the definition, why the curve slopes upward, market supply, every condition of supply, chains of analysis, and the enterprise discussion question worked in full.

CAIE 2281 IGCSE 0455

1. What is supply?

Supply is the amount of a good or service that a producer is willing and able to supply at a given price in a given time period. As with demand, the two words and the time period are the marks.

  • A supply curve is the graphical representation of the price and quantity supplied by producers. It slopes upward from left to right.
  • It slopes upward because there is a positive (direct) relationship between price and quantity supplied: price up, quantity supplied up; price down, quantity supplied down.
  • Why? A higher price makes each unit more profitable, so existing firms expand output and higher-cost firms can now cover their costs. A lower price does the reverse.
  • Supply is always stated per time period: tonnes per week, units per day.
Exam trapProducers supply, consumers demand. Never write "consumers supply 50 units" or "producers demand a higher price". Also, a change in the price of the good itself changes quantity supplied (a movement along the curve), not supply (a shift of the whole curve).

2. Individual and market supply

Individual supply is the quantity one firm will offer at each price. Market supply is the combination of all the individual supply for a good or service, calculated by adding up the individual quantities supplied at each price level.

3. Movements along the supply curve

When the price of the good is the only thing that changes, there is a change in the quantity supplied, shown by a movement along the supply curve.

Worked example 2

The market price of wheat rises after a poor harvest elsewhere in the world. Explain how a wheat farmer in your country responds, and show it on a diagram.

  1. The price of wheat itself has risen: that is a price change, so it is a movement, not a shift.
  2. At the higher price it is profitable to sell more (and to bring marginal land into use).
  3. Move up along the supply curve from a lower point to a higher point, labelling P1, P2, Q1, Q2.

An extension of supply: quantity supplied rises as a movement up along the existing supply curve. The curve does not shift, because nothing except price changed.

4. Conditions of supply (shifts of the curve)

Several factors will change the supply of a good or service irrespective of the price level. Collectively these are the conditions of supply. A change in any of them shifts the entire supply curve instead of causing a movement along it.

Shift or movement, the same two-line testDid the price of this good change? Yes, movement along the curve (extension up, contraction down). No, the whole curve shifts, and you must name the condition of supply: costs, technology, tax, subsidy, number of firms, weather, expectations.

5. Shifts in action: chains of analysis

Supply questions that ask you to "analyse" want a chain, not one sentence. Same four beats every time: what changed, which way the curve shifts, what happens to price, what happens to quantity traded, finished with a context sentence.

Worked example 3

An improvement in technology cuts the cost of making drones. Analyse the effect on the market for drones.

  1. Lower production costs are a condition of supply.
  2. The supply curve shifts right from S1 to S2 (Figure 2).
  3. At the original price, quantity supplied now exceeds quantity demanded: there is excess supply (a surplus).
  4. Producers cut prices to clear the unsold stock, so the price falls.
  5. As price falls, quantity demanded extends along the demand curve and quantity supplied contracts along the new supply curve.
  6. New equilibrium: lower price, higher quantity traded.

Supply shifts right, creating a surplus at the old price; price falls until a new equilibrium is reached with a lower price and a higher quantity of drones.

Worked example 4

The government imposes a higher indirect tax on fuel retailers. Analyse the effect on the market for fuel.

  1. An indirect tax raises the cost per unit for producers.
  2. The supply curve shifts left (a decrease in supply).
  3. At the old price there is now excess demand.
  4. Price rises, quantity demanded contracts, quantity supplied extends along the new curve.
  5. Result: fewer litres sold at a higher price; the tax burden is shared between producers and consumers.

Supply shifts left, so the equilibrium price rises and the equilibrium quantity falls.

Exam tipExaminers reward cause, diagram, consequence in that order. Always finish with both equilibrium changes: price and quantity. "The price falls" on its own is a partial answer.
Exam trap"Supply increases" never means "the price rises because producers want more money". A leftward shift of supply raises price, a rightward shift lowers it. Get the direction right before you write the chain.

6. Supply of enterprise

Enterprise is the factor of production used to organise the other factors and take risks; it is rewarded with profit. The "supply of enterprise" means how many people are willing to set up and run businesses rather than work for a wage.

Influence on the supply of enterpriseWhy it matters
Expected profitsHigher expected profit after costs attracts more people to start businesses.
Political stability and property rightsSecure ownership and predictable government make risk-taking safer.
Taxes and regulationLower taxes on business income and simpler rules raise the reward from enterprise.
Access to financeLoans and microcredit let people with ideas but no capital start firms.
Education and skillsTraining in business and technical skills raises confidence to take risks.
Worked example 5

A government cuts taxes on small businesses and improves access to loans. Analyse the effect on the supply of goods produced by small firms.

  1. After-tax profit rises and more people can finance a start-up, so the supply of enterprise increases.
  2. New firms enter and existing firms expand, so the supply curve shifts right.
  3. At the old price there is excess supply, so price falls and quantity traded rises.

More enterprise means more producers: supply shifts right, giving a lower equilibrium price and a higher equilibrium quantity.

7. Practice set and answers

Do every question on paper first. Open an answer only after you have committed to one.

Set A · Core supply terms

Define supply. State two features your definition must include.
Supply is the amount of a good or service a producer is willing and able to supply at a given price in a given time period. The two essential features are willingness and ability (the firm can cover its costs at that price), and the time period, because supply is always measured per day, week or year.
Explain why the supply curve slopes upward.
There is a positive relationship between price and quantity supplied. A higher price makes each unit more profitable, so firms expand output and higher-cost producers can cover their costs, so a larger quantity is offered. A lower price does the reverse, so the curve rises from left to right.
Distinguish between individual supply and market supply, and state how market supply is calculated.
Individual supply is what one firm offers at each price; market supply is all firms combined. It is calculated by adding up the individual quantities supplied at each price level, row by row through the schedule.
The price of a good rises. Explain, using the terms extension and contraction, what happens to quantity supplied.
It is an extension of supply: a movement up along the existing supply curve to a higher quantity supplied. The curve does not shift, because only the price of the good itself changed.

Set B · Shifts and conditions of supply

01(9)/23/O/N/20(g) Discuss whether or not the supply of enterprise is likely to increase in the Philippines.
Enterprise is the risk-taking factor of production rewarded by profit, so its supply rises when the rewards and security of running a business improve. Arguments for an increase: strong and sustained economic growth creates profitable opportunities; a young, educated population with access to microcredit and business training is willing to take risks; government support for small and medium enterprises, and outsourcing and remittance income, raise the funds available for start-ups. Arguments against: corruption, weak property rights, complex regulation, high taxes and political uncertainty raise the risk and lower the expected reward, which would discourage new entrepreneurs; so would emigration of skilled workers. Conclusion: on balance the supply of enterprise is likely to increase where the Philippines has improved access to finance and stable growth, but only if the government keeps taxes, regulation and corruption low enough for expected profit to remain attractive. (Award yourself the discussion mark by weighing both sides and giving a justified verdict.)
Explain how a rise in the cost of raw materials affects the supply curve for cotton clothing.
Raw materials are an input cost, so production becomes dearer at every price: the supply curve shifts left (a decrease in supply). At the old price there is excess demand, price rises, and quantity traded falls to a new equilibrium.
Explain two causes of an increase in supply of tomatoes.
(1) Good weather gives a bigger harvest, so more tomatoes are offered at every price (supply shifts right). (2) A subsidy to growers (or cheaper fertiliser, or improved storage and transport technology) lowers cost per unit, also shifting supply to the right.
The government pays a subsidy to solar panel producers. Using a chain of analysis, explain the effect on the market for solar panels.
The subsidy lowers producers' costs per unit, so the supply curve shifts right. At the original price there is excess supply, so firms cut prices: the price falls and the quantity demanded extends along the demand curve. New equilibrium: lower price and more solar panels traded.
An increase in the world price of rubber means farmers switch land from palm oil to rubber. What happens to the supply curve for palm oil?
Palm oil and rubber are substitutes in production: the better price of rubber pulls resources away from palm oil, so less palm oil is supplied at every price. The supply curve for palm oil shifts left. At the old price there is excess demand, so the price of palm oil also rises.

Set C · Diagrams and mixed analysis

Describe what is shown on a diagram when producers respond to a fall in the price of their product.
It is a contraction of supply: a movement down along the same supply curve, from a higher price and quantity to a lower price and lower quantity supplied. No shift, because the price of the good itself is the only change.
Explain two reasons why the supply of a good may shift to the right.
(1) An improvement in technology that raises productivity and lowers unit costs. (2) A fall in input costs, or a government subsidy, or new firms entering the market. In each case more is supplied at every price, so the whole curve moves right.
A bakery's flour prices double. Analyse the effect on the market for bread, using a diagram in your answer.
Flour is an input, so higher costs shift the supply curve for bread left. At the old price there is excess demand, so bakers raise prices: price rises and quantity demanded contracts. New equilibrium: higher price, lower quantity of bread traded. (Mark the shift S1 to S2 left, then the price rise along the unchanged demand curve.)
Why might supply increase in one country but not another for the same product?
Because the conditions of supply differ: input costs, wages, technology, taxes and subsidies, the number of producers, climate and the security of property rights vary between countries. If costs are lower or technology better in one country, its supply curve sits further right, so more is supplied at every price.

Answer: D. Better technology lowers cost per unit, so more is supplied at every price. Taxes and higher wages shift supply left; a fall in the product's price is a movement.

Answer: A. Market supply is the horizontal sum of every firm's quantity supplied at each price level.

9. Exam checklist

  • Supply definition includes willing and able, at a given price and in a given time period.
  • Explained the upward slope as a positive relationship between price and quantity supplied.
  • Market supply built by adding firm quantities at each price.
  • Price of the good itself changed? It is a movement (extension up, contraction down), not a shift.
  • Named the condition of supply behind every shift: costs, technology, tax, subsidy, number of firms, weather, expectations, substitutes in production.
  • Chain of analysis written in order: shift the curve, spot excess supply or demand, move the price, state the new equilibrium price and quantity in context.
  • Indirect tax shifts supply left (price up, quantity down); subsidy shifts supply right (price down, quantity up).
  • For enterprise questions: defined enterprise, given both sides, and finished with a justified verdict.
