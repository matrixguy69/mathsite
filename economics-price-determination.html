Chapter 03 · Economics

Price determination: equilibrium and disequilibrium

Where the price actually comes from: equilibrium and the market clearing price, how market forces cure shortages and surpluses, excess demand and excess supply, and reading it all off a demand and supply schedule.

CAIE 2281 IGCSE 0455

1. Equilibrium and the market clearing price

Equilibrium in a market occurs when demand equals supply: the quantity consumers want to buy is exactly the quantity producers want to sell.

equilibrium where QD = QS (at the equilibrium price)
  • At the equilibrium price there is no shortage and no surplus, so there is no pressure for the price to change.
  • This price is called the market clearing price: the price at which sellers are clearing (selling) their stock at an acceptable rate.
  • On a diagram, equilibrium is the point where the demand curve and the supply curve cross. Read the price off the vertical axis and the quantity off the horizontal axis.

Exam tip"When is a market in equilibrium?" gets a short, exact answer: when the quantity demanded equals the quantity supplied, that is when demand equals supply. Add "at the market clearing price" for the bonus phrase.

2. Market forces at work

Markets are rarely left alone for long, but they keep pushing themselves back towards equilibrium. Two market forces do the work:

State of the marketPressure on priceWhat buyers and sellers do
Excess demand (QD above QS)price is pushed upbuyers bid against each other; sellers can raise prices
Excess supply (QS above QD)price is pushed downsellers discount unsold stock; buyers wait for cuts
Equilibriumno pressurestock clears at an acceptable rate
Worked example 2

In the schedule from Worked example 1, the price is $20. Explain how the market returns to equilibrium, using a chain of analysis.

  1. At $20, QD = 900 and QS = 100, so there is excess demand of 800 units.
  2. Shops sell out and disappointed buyers offer more, so the price rises.
  3. As price rises, quantity demanded contracts along the demand curve and quantity supplied extends along the supply curve.
  4. The gap closes until QD = QS at $40.

Excess demand pushes the price up from $20 to the market clearing price of $40, where 500 units are traded.

The direction checkShortage, price up. Surplus, price down. If your answer has the price moving the wrong way, re-read the question: you have probably mixed up demand and supply.

3. Market disequilibrium: excess demand

Disequilibrium is any situation where the market is not at the point where demand equals supply. Excess demand occurs when the demand is greater than the supply.

  • It can occur when prices are set too low (for example by a government price ceiling), or when demand is so high that supply cannot keep up (a sudden trend, a shortage of raw materials, panic buying).
  • On a diagram it is the horizontal gap between the demand curve and the supply curve at a price below equilibrium.
  • The symptoms are queues, waiting lists, rationing, empty shelves and black markets at even higher prices.
Exam trap"Excess demand" is the gap, not a shift of anything. On a diagram, shade or label the horizontal distance between the two curves at the disequilibrium price, and always say whether the price is below or above equilibrium.

4. Market disequilibrium: excess supply

Excess supply occurs when the supply is greater than the demand.

  • It can occur when prices are set too high (for example a minimum price), or when demand falls unexpectedly (a change in tastes, a substitute becoming cheaper, a recession).
  • On a diagram it is the horizontal gap between the supply curve and the demand curve at a price above equilibrium.
  • The symptoms are unsold stock, discounting, sales, rising storage costs and, in the long run, firms leaving the market.
Worked example 3

During the later stages of the pandemic the market for face masks was in disequilibrium: shops were full of unsold boxes. Using the trainer schedule from Worked example 1, analyse a market stuck at $60.

  1. At $60, QS = 900 but QD = 100, so there is excess supply of 800 units.
  2. Producers are holding unsold stock, so they cut prices and run sales.
  3. As price falls, quantity demanded extends and quantity supplied contracts.
  4. The surplus disappears at the market clearing price of $40.

Excess supply pushes the price down from $60 until demand equals supply at $40, clearing the unsold stock.

Exam tipReal-world context earns application marks. Face masks during the pandemic are the classic case: first demand surged beyond supply (prices rose), then demand collapsed while supply stayed high (excess supply, falling prices). Name the product and the direction every time.

5. Demand and supply schedules

A demand and supply schedule shows the quantity demanded and the quantity supplied of a product at different price levels, usually in one table. Schedules can be used to identify equilibrium and disequilibrium without drawing a single curve.

excess demand = QD − QS (QD greater)  ·  excess supply = QS − QD (QS greater)

A weekly schedule for a product, such as the YEEZY Boost 700 schedule in your notes, works exactly like this: read across each row to see the state of the market at that price, and read down to see how the state changes as price rises.

Worked example 4

State the market situation at $50 in the table above, and explain what will happen next.

  1. At $50, QS = 700 and QD = 300.
  2. 700 − 300 = 400 units unsold: excess supply of 400.
  3. The price is above equilibrium, so market forces push it down.
  4. It falls towards $40, where the columns match.

At $50 the market has a surplus of 400 units, so price falls to the equilibrium of $40.

6. Equilibrium chains of analysis

Full-mark chains have the same skeleton: state the disequilibrium, explain the price pressure, describe the movements along both curves, name the new equilibrium, finish in context. Use it whenever a question asks how a market moves somewhere.

Worked example 5

Explain how a market moves from disequilibrium to equilibrium after a sudden rise in demand.

  1. Demand shifts right, so at the old price quantity demanded exceeds quantity supplied: excess demand.
  2. Buyers bid up the price, or sellers raise it in response to the queues.
  3. As price rises, quantity demanded contracts along the new (higher) demand curve and quantity supplied extends along the supply curve.
  4. The excess demand shrinks to zero where the curves now cross.

The market moves to a new equilibrium at a higher price and a higher quantity, with no shortage left.

Worked example 6

A harvest failure reduces the supply of oranges. Explain the effect on the market for orange juice.

  1. Fewer oranges mean supply of juice shifts left (a fall in supply).
  2. At the old price there is now excess demand.
  3. Price rises; quantity demanded contracts, quantity supplied extends along the new curve.
  4. New equilibrium: higher price, lower quantity of orange juice traded.

A leftward shift of supply creates excess demand, so the price of orange juice rises until demand equals supply at a lower quantity.

Exam trapDo not say "demand and supply both change" unless the question says so. A shift in demand alone still moves price and quantity traded, because the market travels along the unchanged supply curve. Name which curve shifted and which curve you are moving along.

7. Practice set and answers

Do every question on paper first. Open an answer only after you have committed to one.

Set A · Equilibrium and disequilibrium

Q3(a)/21/M/J/19(a) When is a market in equilibrium?
A market is in equilibrium when demand equals supply, that is when the quantity demanded equals the quantity supplied at the market clearing price. At this price there is no shortage and no surplus, so there is no tendency for the price to change.
Q2(a)/23/O/N/22(a) Define market disequilibrium.
Market disequilibrium is a situation in which the quantity demanded is not equal to the quantity supplied, so the market is either in excess demand (QD greater than QS) or in excess supply (QS greater than QD), and market forces are pushing the price towards equilibrium.
Q5(b)/22/O/N/23(b) Explain how a market moves from disequilibrium to equilibrium.
In excess demand, buyers compete for the limited supply, so the price rises; as it rises, quantity demanded contracts and quantity supplied extends, closing the shortage. In excess supply, sellers discount the unsold stock, so the price falls; as it falls, quantity demanded extends and quantity supplied contracts, closing the surplus. In both cases the market moves back to the point where demand equals supply, the equilibrium (market clearing) price, and the excess disappears.
Q3(b)/22/M/J/19(b) Explain how market forces will eliminate a surplus and a shortage.
A surplus (excess supply) means quantity supplied exceeds quantity demanded at the current price. Producers cut prices to sell the unsold stock: the price falls, quantity supplied contracts and quantity demanded extends, until the surplus is gone. A shortage (excess demand) means quantity demanded exceeds quantity supplied. Buyers bid up the price and sellers can charge more: the price rises, quantity demanded contracts and quantity supplied extends, until the shortage is gone. Both market forces return price to the level where demand equals supply.

Set B · Schedules and diagrams

At a price of $5, a schedule shows QD = 400 and QS = 600. State the position of the market and explain what will happen to the price.
Quantity supplied exceeds quantity demanded by 200 units, so the market is in excess supply of 200 (a surplus). Producers will cut prices to clear the unsold stock, so the price falls; quantity demanded extends and quantity supplied contracts until QD = QS at the new, lower equilibrium price.
Explain what is meant by the market clearing price, and show how to find it on a demand and supply diagram.
The market clearing price is the price at which sellers clear (sell) their stock at an acceptable rate, that is where quantity demanded equals quantity supplied. On a diagram it is found at the point where the demand and supply curves intersect, then read off the vertical (price) axis. The quantity at that point, read off the horizontal axis, is the equilibrium quantity.
The price in a market is below the equilibrium price. Using a diagram, explain what happens next.
Below equilibrium, quantity demanded is greater than quantity supplied: there is excess demand, shown as the horizontal gap between the curves at that price (Figure 2). The shortage puts upward pressure on price. As the price rises, quantity demanded contracts along the demand curve and quantity supplied extends along the supply curve, until the gap closes at the equilibrium price and quantity.

Set C · Applying it

During the pandemic the price of face masks first rose sharply and later fell. Explain both stages using demand and supply analysis.
Stage 1: demand for masks shifted far right (health fears) while supply could not keep up, so at the old price there was excess demand; prices were bid up until the new, higher equilibrium. Stage 2: demand fell back as fears eased, while new producers and expanded capacity kept supply high, so at the going price there was now excess supply; sellers discounted unsold stock, the price fell, and the market settled at a lower equilibrium price and quantity.
Explain why the price of fresh strawberries is usually higher in winter than in summer, using market equilibrium.
In winter the supply shifts left (fewer are in season, greenhouse production costs more), while demand stays similar. At the old price there is excess demand, so the price is pushed up to a new equilibrium at a higher price and lower quantity. In summer, good weather shifts supply right, creating temporary excess supply at the winter price, so the price falls to a lower equilibrium.
State one condition of demand and one condition of supply that could change, and give the effect on the equilibrium price and quantity for electric cars.
Condition of demand: rising incomes (or successful advertising) shifts demand right, creating excess demand, so price and quantity both rise. Condition of supply: cheaper batteries (or a subsidy) shifts supply right, creating excess supply at the old price, so price falls while quantity rises. If both shifted right, quantity would rise for sure and the price effect would depend on which shift is larger.

8. Quick quiz

Click an option to check your answer.

MCQ 1 A market is in equilibrium when:

Answer: B. Equilibrium is where demand equals supply, at the market clearing price.

MCQ 2 The market clearing price is the price at which:

Answer: C. At the market clearing price everything offered is sold, so there is no shortage or surplus.

MCQ 3 The price in a market is below equilibrium. What results?

Answer: A. Below equilibrium, QD is greater than QS: a shortage, and buyers bid the price up.

MCQ 4 A surplus in a market means:

Answer: D. A surplus is excess supply, which occurs at a price above equilibrium and pushes the price down.

MCQ 5 A demand and supply schedule shows:

Answer: B. The schedule lists QD and QS at each price, which is enough to spot both equilibrium and disequilibrium.

9. Exam checklist

  • Equilibrium defined as demand equals supply (QD = QS).
  • Market clearing price named: the price at which sellers clear their stock at an acceptable rate.
  • Equilibrium located at the crossing point of the curves, with price and quantity read off both axes.
  • Excess demand (QD above QS, price too low) and excess supply (QS above QD, price too high) defined and distinguished.
  • Diagram gaps drawn as the horizontal distance between the curves at the disequilibrium price.
  • Market forces written in the right direction: shortage pushes price up, surplus pushes price down.
  • Chains complete: disequilibrium, price pressure, movements along both curves, new equilibrium, context sentence.
  • Schedule questions answered by comparing the QD and QS columns row by row, then stating the size of any excess.
